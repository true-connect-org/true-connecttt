**The hook:** Most brands advertise their product. Amul comments on the news, and the product comes along for the ride.

## The campaign

In 1966, Amul asked the agency Advertising and Sales Promotion (ASP) for a mascot. Its managing director, Sylvester daCunha, worked with illustrator Eustace Fernandes to create the Amul girl: a cheeky child in a polka-dot dress, paired with the line "Utterly Butterly Delicious".

Within a few years the ads turned topical. Instead of repeating a product message, each new ad reacted to something people were already talking about: an election, a cricket win, a film release, a scandal. A short pun, a simple drawing, and Amul butter somewhere in the frame. The ads often appeared within days of the event, and the format has continued for decades.

## Why it worked

1. **It borrowed attention that already existed.** People were already discussing the news. Amul simply joined the conversation.
2. **One character, endless stories.** The Amul girl stayed the same, so every new ad built on the recognition of the last one.
3. **Wit made it shareable.** A clever pun is something people repeat, screenshot and forward. The brand travels for free.
4. **Speed was part of the brand.** Reacting within days made Amul feel current, even as the campaign itself grew older.
5. **The product stayed simple.** Butter appears in almost every ad, but never as a hard sell.

## The result

Industry voices widely describe it as one of India's longest-running ad campaigns, and the Amul girl remains one of the country's most recognised brand characters. The topicals are now a regular part of how many people follow the news.

## How a Kerala business can use this

- **Create a recurring character or format.** A mascot, a signature visual or a fixed post style makes every piece instantly recognisable.
- **React to local moments.** Onam, a Kerala Blasters match, monsoon updates or a big local event are chances to join conversations people already care about.
- **Keep it quick and light.** A same-day reel or post with a smart line often outperforms a polished ad that arrives a week late.
- **Stay tasteful.** Topical humour works when it is clever, not cruel. Avoid tragedies and sensitive issues.
